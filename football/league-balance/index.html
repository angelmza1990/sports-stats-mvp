League balance

Which leagues belong to two clubs, and which are anyone's to win? Every national champion of seventeen leagues, from England's first season in 1888 to today, compared on the same measures.

How even is each league?

The effective number of champions is how many equally successful clubs would share the titles the same way: one over the sum of each club's squared share of the titles. A league whose titles all went to one club scores 1; one where ten clubs won the same number scores 10.

Decade by decade

The share of each decade's titles won by that decade's two most successful clubs. Darker means fewer clubs shared the titles. Hover or tap a cell for its champions.

Is football getting more predictable?

The effective number of champions in each league's last 20 titles, title by title, so that leagues with one and two champions a year compare on the same window. Pick up to four leagues.

A value of 2 means two clubs shared the last 20 titles evenly, or one club dominated with a few others; 20 would be twenty different champions in a row.

The longest reigns

The most consecutive titles won by one club in any of the seventeen leagues. Where a league crowns two champions a year, each tournament counts.

The most dominant clubs

Each league's most successful club and its share of all the titles the league has awarded.

The last club in each league to win its first title, and how long ago that was.

Data: the champions of each league page on this site (), from Wikipedia's lists of national champions (CC BY-SA 4.0).

Notes: every recognised title counts once, including the amateur eras, the split associations of early Argentine football and the short tournaments of Mexico, Colombia, Paraguay and Argentina, each of which crowns its own champion. A season shared by two champions counts as one title for each. The effective number of champions is the inverse of the Herfindahl–Hirschman index of the clubs' title shares; it grows with the number of titles a league has awarded, which is why the trend chart uses a fixed window of 20 titles.
