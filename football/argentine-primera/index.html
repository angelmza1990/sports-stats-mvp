The amateur era and its splits

From 1891 to 1934 the league was amateur. Three times, a rival association broke away and ran its own championship, so two champions were crowned in the same year. The AFA recognises both.

Data: Wikipedia, List of Argentine Primera División champions (CC BY-SA 4.0), following the AFA's official list. Updated automatically twice a year.

Notes: the professional era starts with the Liga Argentina de Football in 1931; the amateur championships that continued until 1934 count as amateur titles. Domestic cups (such as the Copa de la Liga Profesional) are not league titles. Top scorers come from Wikipedia's list of Primera División top scorers; tournaments with joint top scorers list each of them. The all-time tables come from Spanish Wikipedia (based on RSSSF) and are shown as published: two points per win until 1994–95 and three since, with deducted points subtracted. A few totals in the source don't add up exactly. The 1891 title was shared by St. Andrew's and Old Caledonians. Clubs that no longer exist or have no crest available show their initials.
